This article is an in-depth look into which players offer the most potential value at each price point, chiefly focusing on the players that typically excel at accumulating DefCon points.

I’ll also take a look at bonus point magnets at the bottom of this piece.

DefCon Leaders: Defenders

The most notable talking point ahead of this season is the fact that the vast majority of the best-performing DefCon assets from last season have either changed team or changed manager. This adds a degree of uncertainty to predicting the nature of this season.

Not only that, but the majority of top performers have received a price increase, too.

So, my current thinking is perhaps to instead gamble on a few new potential DefCon gems.

Immediately, the £5.0m bracket looks like the sweet spot. I think there is so much potential at that price across the board.

VUSKOVIC + MUHAREMOVIC

It is new boys Luka Vuskovic and Tarik Muharemovic (both £5.0m) who really catch my eye, both boasting extremely impressive DEFCON numbers and attacking data from their previous clubs. 

I’m very keen just to go straight into the season with these two players. I think the upside is potentially huge, and they could match those star men from last season who are now in the £6.0m bracket. 

Muharemovic looks to be by far the safer pick of the two. He was straight into the left side of the Leeds back three and seamlessly settled in over pre-season (not to mention his bicycle kick goal!). Leeds look even stronger defensively with the addition of James Trafford (£5.0m), and I can also see Muharemovic chipping in with some attacking returns.

Vuskovic, on the other hand, may not be a week-to-week starter at first. However, the fact that he started the last two friendlies gives me a lot more confidence in his likelihood of a start in Gameweek 1, at least. 

The upside with Vuskovic looks huge. He hit DefCon in just 60 minutes in the last friendly and has already had a couple of good chances from corners in pre-season. His goal record from centre back is incredible for his age (17 goals in 91 games). I’d be shocked if we don’t see a good few big hauls from him this season! This is the main reason why I’m likely to just risk him from the off.

Of course, all of these promising numbers may not translate to FPL; I just believe it could be worth just taking the gamble for the huge potential upside. There is an early Wildcard as cover, and a lot of people will likely be using that chip in the opening six Gameweeks regardless.

Which defenders had the highest floor score last season?

This is an important consideration in DefCon. 

Players may repeatedly hit the defensive contribution threshold but if they concede significantly or get booked a lot, it slowly loses its value. Look at Maxime Esteve last season: 38 DefCon points but a shortage of shut-outs meant he averaged a miserable 2.5 points per game.

4+ points is the dream target for all defenders. Either a simple clean sheet, an attacking return, or DefCon points with just one goal conceded.

Dan Ballard (£5.0m) and Jaka Bijol (£4.5m) posted quite remarkable numbers from last year, particularly in home games. They’re names to consider if you’re planning a player rotation that involves a lot of home matches.

The Bournemouth defence was ridiculously consistent across the board, but this one maybe needs a bit of time to see how the new manager gets on.

We have the perfect entry point for Bournemouth assets, too, in Gameweek 7 (or on a Wildcard just before that). Adrien Truffert (£5.5m) and James Hill (£5.5m) are very much on my watchlist until then. They could still offer tremendous value, even with price rises, should they replicate the numbers from last season.

Defenders who hit DefCon whilst keeping a clean sheet

This is definitely one of the more underrated considerations for players, and particularly assets that you hope will hit DefCon a lot. 

Some players will hit DefCon points in a large portion of games. But they might also scarcely reach the threshold in matches in which they keep a clean sheet; if you’re in a mismatch against a Premier League minnow, for instance, there may be less defensive work to do.

Ballard and Bijol boasted impressive numbers last season. They racked up the DefCon quite regularly, even whilst keeping clean sheets. Those two (then newly promoted) defences put up resilient clean sheet performances throughout, resulting in a dream combination for FPL numbers. 

Perhaps we will see the same this season with the newly promoted teams? In particular, whichever club looks to be the most solid at the back. Ipswich Town, on paper, had the best defensive data last season out of the three teams, but the bookies/projections fancy Coventry City to ship the least in 2026/27.

The ones to watch for me are the Nottingham Forest assets. We all know how amazing they would have been for DefCon in 2024/25, often topping many pre-season charts ahead of the DefCon introduction.

If they improve defensively under Oliver Glasner, then we could be looking at some big DefCon returns combined with clean sheets. If the Austrian’s impact on the Crystal Palace defenders was anything to go by, then we might be in luck here with the likes of Nikola Milenkovic (£5.5m) and Murillo (£5.5m). 

DefCon Leaders: Midfielders

DefCon-amassing midfielders perhaps go under the radar a bit more than their all-tackling peers in defence.

However, if you get a player that can accumulate a fair share of both attacking returns + DefCon points, you have a very consistent and dependable source of points.

The £6.0m bracket is looking particularly healthy.

James Garner (£6.0m) would be the perfect Gameweek 1 pick if not for his surgery – so who is a decent alternative?

Anton Stach (£6.0m) and Enzo Le Fee (£6.0m) are the other two that catch the eye to begin with. Both of them cover so many avenues to points at the bracket with chance creation, set pieces and a share of penalty-taking duties in Le Fee’s case. I believe that these assets are ones that you won’t ever be in a huge rush to sell, either, which is ideal if you plan to play a more patient style.

Incidentally, Mamadou Sangare (£5.5m), who recently joined Brentford, isn’t on this graphic. But he had a whopping 65% DefCon hit rate at his previous club Lens and chipped in with seven attacking returns! If he even remotely replicates this, then he could become one of the best-value picks in the game, much like Elliot Anderson (£6.5m) last season.

Sangare is someone that I’m also quite keen to gamble on from the off, especially after seeing him fit into the team extremely well over the past couple of friendlies. 

Which midfielders had the highest floor score last season?

It’s nice to see here how the DefCon picks compare to the best premium attacking midfielders from last season, on a consistency basis.

The big hitters will get bigger hauls more often, but the DefCon guys can rack up very consistent small returns. These become invaluable over the course of the season, especially in weeks where you ideally don’t want to make a transfer or have other fires to put out. 

Sangare was incorporated into this chart after his move and you can see how highly he would have ranked with his DefCon numbers last season in France.

Stach stands out as amazing value once again. He registered 11 attacking returns, despite missing a third of the season, plus he has the ability to hit DefCon. He’s someone you could quite easily play in any fixture if needs be.

Granit Xhaka (£5.5m) also comes up with very respectable numbers at the price point, and he’s in fewer than 5% of teams. He’s one you can count on to play very consistent minutes, take some set pieces and chip in with occasional attacking returns – combined with a respectable 41% DefCon hit rate from last season.

But most interestingly, on the centre-back side, a large number of the best DefCon opponents do still have their manager from last year. So, a few assumptions can be made off the back of this!

These two less appealing fixtures are sandwiched in between a very nice run of games for them, meaning all the opening games seem viable for their midfield picks, such as Iliman Ndiaye (£6.0m). 

Which players gain bonus points the most whilst hitting just one return?

Note: A return here is classified as one of: goal, assist, clean sheet or penalty save.

A little additional section, away from DefCon, is a dive into some of the more bonus point-prone players. This takes into account the new changes to the Bonus Points System (BPS) (all data here has incorporated those tweaks).

Anyone can hit bonus points with 2+ returns – but the likelihood of achieving bonus when registering just a single return can really extend the value of an option.

Strikers generally perform well in the bonus stakes with just the one goal, but Igor Thiago (£8.0m) is one who does particularly badly. He fouls a lot, misses lots of chances, and scores penalty goals, which are worth half the bps of a normal striker goal.

Defenders generally share the bonus with other defenders when they keep a clean sheet, so it’s tough for them to consistently rack up extras if they have just one return. Neco Williams (£5.0m) and Truffert are quite impressive cases here, particularly the Bournemouth man. There were 18 games in which he registered a single return, and he hit bonus points in half of those.

Florian Wirtz (£7.0m) and Bruno Guimaraes (£6.5m) also boast eye-catching numbers. They often hit bonus after just one return, and it’s often higher than one bonus point.

Ethan Ampadu demonstrates some fantastic consistency at just £5.5m, albeit from a small sample of returns. You might not get too many goals and assists but when they do come, you can almost count on bonus points. Failing that, he also has very tidy DefCon prospects and will tick over in the meantime. 

Matheus Cunha (£8.0m) was incredibly consistent for bonus points, given the vast amount of single returns he had. In fact, he didn’t have a single game with two or more returns last season, but he made up for that by accumulating bonus points on nearly 80% of the 14 occasions he scored or assisted. 

Bruno Fernandes (£12.0m) and Declan Rice (£7.5m) fare well in the bonus metrics, too.

Final Thoughts

Now, all of these stats won’t directly correlate to the new 2026/27 season.

But players also don’t change overnight. So, a lot of their tendencies, whether it’s their game style that is very DefCon-orientated or their bonus point magnetism, won’t dramatically alter.

During pre-season, last year’s data is the main source of information that we have to go off.

And what we learned last year was that embracing DefCon points – whether you like their introduction to FPL or not – often reaps rewards.

So, it will be extremely beneficial to try and hit those top performers as early as possible this time around.

Hopefully, this article goes some way to helping you do this!
