MORE ABOUT THE SCOUT SQUAD

The focus is only on the upcoming Gameweek with the Scout Squad, so there’s no medium-term planning involved.

The players who get the most votes are much more likely to make the Scout Picks, although the final XI can’t exceed £83.0m – so occasionally, cheaper alternatives have to be found.

Each of our writers must meet the following requirements for this feature:

  • At least one sub-£5.0m goalkeeper
  • At least one sub-£5.0m defender
  • At least one sub-£6.0m midfielder
  • At least one sub-£7.0m forward
  • No more than three players from the same club

SCOUT SQUAD: BEST FPL PLAYERS FOR GAMEWEEK 1

  • MOST PICKS: Gabriel Magalhaes, Riccardo Calafiori, Bryan Mbeumo, Bruno Fernandes, Christos Tzolis, Erling Haaland, Alexander Isak, Joao Pedro (four), Pascal Gross, Igor Thiago (three)

MARC SAID…

Welcome back, everyone! I hope you’ve all had a lush summer and feel fully rested.

The Premier League has been like that gif from ‘Community’ where Troy briefly leaves the room and comes back to find everything on fire. Absolute carnage. Truly, anything could happen between second and 17th place, due to the many new managers, plus teams like Aston Villa and Newcastle being firmly put back in their place after having the audacity to be ambitious.

Arsenal will likely be champions again by default, so let’s go heavy on them versus Coventry. Being able to select Gabriel Magalhaes without considering his price tag feels nice. But the rest of their back four is too hard to call beyond the first few weeks

Riccardo Calafiori’s 2025/26 minutes per shot rate (53.6) was superb, and then he scored in the Community Shield. New signing Christos Tzolis created their other two goals and, after setting up seven in total, looks ready to go.

Last season’s 23 assists at Club Brugge were two more than Bruno Fernandes, someone about to visit Hull. I’ll triple up on Man United, as the playoff winners somehow had the Championship’s second-worst expected goals conceded (xGC, 82.69) tally.

Bryan Mbeumo is scoring summer goals, and I don’t think managers should worry about Saturday’s non-appearance. At the back, there’s Harry Maguire, whose last campaign racked up a low one goal and four shots on target. Yet the centre-back netted past AC Milan, a timely reminder of his capabilities.

My main problem with a Gameweek 1 Bench Boost is that not many defences have an appealing fixture beyond Arsenal and Man United. I don’t really ‘get’ the Antonin Kinsky ownership, or the popular rotation between him and Bart Verbruggen – their schedules peak at the same time! Maybe he’ll collect some saves at Brentford, though Igor Thiago will fancy his chances.

Meanwhile, I back Nottingham Forest to score, but Leeds’ Tarik Muharemovic averaged 10.80 DefCon actions per 90 last season, then scored an overhead kick at the weekend. There’s even a strange Robert Sanchez pick. Chelsea didn’t register a single clean sheet after Gameweek 22, but they have a trip to weak-looking Fulham.

At Portman Road, Kjell Scherpen and Trai Hume go head-to-head. Sunderland vastly outdid their underlying stats in 2025/26, but I see them in a scrap this time. However, the possibility of Hume beginning as a right winger is tempting: he netted in Gameweek 38, and during both of their latest friendlies.

Further upfield, low-priced Pascal Gross should be a good enabler, and Erling Haaland is an obvious choice for me, despite what happened at Wembley. Looking across the first six matches of all four Man City seasons, he’s scored 37 goals in 24 matches, including five hat-tricks and 11 double-digit hauls. If anyone can stop Bournemouth’s long unbeaten run, it’s still the Norwegian.

Like many, I’d pursue the front three of him, Joao Pedro and Dominic Calvert-Lewin. It was surprising to see the Brazilian remain at £7.5m, and he’s since bagged eight pre-season goals. Calvert-Lewin’s streakiness is frustrating to own, but he finished the campaign up in third for expected goals (xG, 15.81) and fourth for box shots (68).

Finally, I think Liverpool will have a great time in Newcastle, who’ve been decimated and now lack leadership. It’ll be an emotional occasion for several reasons, but Alexander Isak should haul amongst the deafening boos. As Florian Wirtz seems to be almost alongside him as a second striker, I prefer owning him rather than Dominik Szoboszlai.

SAM SAID…

I feel like this summer has disappeared in the blink of an eye. I guess that’s what happens when you have World Cup Fantasy deadlines for most of it and then an FPL launch just a few days after the World Cup final! So, here we are again with our first picks for a new season. Often these are my favourite picks as we are relying on gut feeling as well as pre-season form, but this year we have the added drama of several new Premier League managers, late returners from the World Cup and a really busy and active summer transfer window. Without further ado, let’s get into it.

I’m not sure what to make of Aston Villa for this opening weekend. They’ve lost a number of key assets already, including Lucas Digne, Morgan Rogers and Youri Tielemans, while there is a lot of speculation about players like Ollie Watkins and Ezri Konsa, too. With that in mind, I’ve opted for budget-friendly Bart Verbruggen between the sticks. I don’t think he is necessarily the best goalkeeper for Gameweek 1 but with triple-ups on Manchester United and Arsenal elsewhere, I’m deprived of the option of picking Senne Lammens and David Raya.

Continuing with the goalkeeper picks, Robin Roefs had an outstanding first Premier League season for Sunderland and faces newly promoted Ipswich this weekend. Meanwhile, Spurs’ new number one, Antonin Kinsky, has had a good pre-season with two clean sheets in three matches. He would have also picked up a save point (to go with the clean sheet) in Spurs’ last pre-season friendly, had it been a Premier League game!

Since FPL launched in July, I have pondered how to make Gabriel Magalhaes work in my actual FPL team. Sadly, I just can’t build a squad I like that contains him. However, he is clearly the best defender in the game and a fixture at home to Coventry under the lights made him the obvious first pick here. Teammate Riccardo Calafiori was really impressive in the Community Shield on Sunday. If Arsenal were in possession, he was attacking. Given the great home fixture, he could easily register a double-digit haul!

Manchester United’s Harry Maguire seems to be the answer to the question, ‘Who will Bruno Fernandes aim for at set pieces?’. The defender scored from Fernandes’ assist in United’s final pre-season friendly, and with what looks like the game of the weekend against Hull, he has potential at both ends.

Tarik Muharemovic‘s inclusion here honestly isn’t based on his bicycle kick goal on Saturday afternoon, but more to do with his likely upside from DefCon points. Nottingham Forest will attack, as we have seen in pre-season, but with the defensive contributions and additional threat from set pieces, I think he’s a good option even if the clean sheet doesn’t stick.

Andy Robertson rounds off my defender picks. Destiny Udogie’s injury-enforced absence in pre-season means the Scot is now almost certainly set to start for Spurs. He registered an assist and kept a clean sheet in Spurs’ final friendly against Hoffenheim. Whilst he might be a long-term rotation issue when Udogie and even Micky van de Ven are back, for Gameweek 1, he looks great.

Manchester United dominate the midfield, given the fixture and the pre-season form. Pairing Bruno Fernandes with Bryan Mbeumo was a no-brainer for Gameweek 1, and, spoiler alert, they will likely be in my Gameweek 2 picks, too!

Christos Tzolis was brilliant in the Community Shield on Sunday. With a home fixture against Coventry on Friday evening, he could have a double-digit return in him on his first FPL appearance.

Whilst there is a lot of chatter around Florian Wirtz and his role in the ‘number 10’ slot for Liverpool, I still prefer the Dominik Szoboszlai pick, with his set-piece threat and the potential to add a cheeky couple of defensive contribution points if he is in the double pivot.

In the £5.5m bracket, I much prefer the Mamadou Sangare pick longer term. However, for this fixture, with Brighton at home against a Villa side lacking many of their first XI from last season, I have opted for the potentially penalty-taking, and definitely set-piece-taking, Pascal Gross.

Erling Haaland, barring an injury, might as well lock himself into these picks for the next 38 weeks! He can lock Joao Pedro in with him. I have the Chelsea forward down as this season’s Golden Boot winner, and I can see him getting off to a great start against Fulham on Monday evening.

Last season was a write-off for Alexander Isak but he was a phenomenal pick during the 2024/25 season, lest we forget. Isak looks set to be the key beneficiary at the heart of that Liverpool attack, especially with Hugo Ekitike out with a long-term injury.

Dominic Calvert-Lewin has continued pre-season as he left off last season, and even when he isn’t scoring himself, he is assisting his teammates.

Meanwhile, with Dominic Solanke seemingly unavailable, having missed the final pre-season friendlies, Richarlison looks set to lead the line for Spurs at a ground in which they historically perform well. Brentford have only beaten Spurs once in 10 Premier League meetings between the sides.

TOM SAID…

Arsenal are the only side that supply three outfield players in my squad – they face newly-promoted Coventry City.

Bukayo Saka is their best attacker when he’s fully fit, but he isn’t a likely starter after the World Cup, so I’ve instead opted for Christos Tzolis. He comes with medium-term caveats (Arsenal are heavily rumoured to be after a new left winger), but he’s looked potent in pre-season, with one goal and six assists in six friendly matches.

I also like Riccardo Calafiori, who netted the opener and was roaming all over the pitch in Sunday’s Community Shield win over Manchester City. He’s part of a defensive double-up with Gabriel Magalhaes.

Worryingly for them, Hull City conceded more goals than 19 other Championship teams last season. Bruno Fernandes is a no-brainer, while I’m expecting Bryan Mbeumo to play up front at the MKM Stadium. In Benjamin Sesko’s absence, he led the line and scored a brace in the 2-1 summer friendly win over Atletico Madrid, plus the equaliser in the draw with Paris Saint-Germain. He also supplied an assist against Leeds United recently, so I’m more than happy to side with him.

I ideally wanted to include Harry Maguire on my list, but there are more stand-out defenders than goalkeepers this week. It’s why Senne Lammens is my representative from the Manchester United backline.

New Crystal Palace head coach Pierre Sage has already spoken about how unready his squad seem to be for the new season. I think Jordan Pickford and James Tarkowski could subsequently keep a clean sheet, while I’m also willing to punt on Thierno Barry as my fifth forward. He could score quite a few more goals this season.

I’ve selected Erling Haaland as my primary forward option, despite a low-key run out against Arsenal last weekend. We’ve been here before, however, so I remain optimistic about his prospects against Bournemouth, who I think will give up plenty of chances under Marco Rose. Nico O’Reilly, who will push up from left-back into central attacking positions under Enzo Maresca, is alongside him.

I think there’ll be goals at St James’ Park on Sunday, given the respective approaches of Matthias Jaissle and Andoni Iraola. There were plenty when the two sides met last season, with 3-2 and 4-1 wins for Liverpool. Dominik Szoboszlai looks like he’ll be a perfect fit for ‘Iraola-ball’, while I think Alexander Isak could be the difference maker for the Reds this season. I’m hopeful he’ll be on penalties, too.

A clean sheet might be a stretch for new boys Ipswich Town, but there isn’t really a £4.5m goalkeeper who stands out this week. Bart Verbruggen, for example, faces an Aston Villa side who have enjoyed an excellent recent record against Brighton and Hove Albion (hence the John McGinn pick as my budget midfielder!), while I can’t see Antonin Kinsky shutting out Igor Thiago and co over 90 minutes. So I’ve opted for Kjell Scherpen at home to a Sunderland side who struggled for goals on the road last season. 

NEALE SAID…

More than ever, it feels like we are going into the new season stabbing in the dark. The arrival of nine new top-flight managers could render much of last season’s data obsolete, while the World Cup stragglers are making medium-term squad planning tricky. That’s on top of the usual summer disarray caused by new signings!

This Scout Squad selection plays it fairly safe then, with 17 of the 18 still on the books of clubs they were at last season. And all bar four of those are playing under the same head coach.

The one ‘new boy’ is Christos Tzolis, and even his inclusion is partly dictated by the usual suspects – ie Bukayo Saka, Declan Rice – being minutes risks after late returns from international duty. Still, he’s fitted in seamlessly off the left, with the Community Shield the biggest test he’s passed so far. His corners look great, which is ideal for Set Piece FC, while Monaco got quite a bit of joy down Coventry’s right on Friday (albeit without scoring), which potentially bodes well for the Greek winger.

With slight uncertainty up top (Kai Havertz led the line at Wembley, but Viktor Gyokeres is the in-house flat-track bully), I’ve completed the Arsenal triple-up with Gabriel Magalhaes and Riccardo Calafiori, who offer a supplementary attacking threat in different ways – the former from set plays, the latter with his lung-busting runs upfield.

The ‘Hull were 23rd for xGC in the Championship’ line will start to drift off into the redundant stat ether soon, as they’re embarking on a Skins-style recasting of their entire XI via the transfer market. However, while the new boys settle in and others prepare to arrive, eight of their line-up in the final pre-season friendly came up with them from the second tier. The vulnerabilities will still be there, should Man Utd ‘see off the new ball’ (ie the buoyant atmosphere at a newly promoted club), so an ‘out of position’ Bryan Mbeumo and Scout Picks staple Bruno Fernandes ought to make hay. I’m not hot on any of the United defenders, so Senne Lammens, who is invariably kept busy – ergo, save points – by the unconvincing bunch in front of him, gets the nod.

Sunderland probably have the toughest test of the sides facing a newly promoted club. We saw how a hyped-up home support could influence proceedings in Gameweek 1 of 2025/26, when the Black Cats themselves marked their Premier League return with a home win. Leeds did the same.

But the Black Cats and Regis Le Bris are not a naive lot, and again, if they can ride out the opening half an hour, they should be too strong. Luke O’Nien is a perfectly good Bench Boost option this weekend but, if money is no object, he doesn’t offer nearly the same points potential as Dan Ballard. The Northern Irishman averaged 10.9 defensive contributions per 90 minutes in 2025/26, hitting the required threshold in 58 per cent of his starts, and also delivered four attacking returns. Brian Brobbey, meanwhile, is probably the pick of the £6.0m strikers for this week in isolation.

There are some tough-to-call fixtures this weekend, such as Nottingham Forest v Leeds United and Brentford v Tottenham Hotspur, but other seemingly close contests may be tipping in favour of one side.

Aston Villa and Newcastle United have been asset-stripped this summer, for example, with the former set to lose Ezri Konsa and battling to hold on to Ollie Watkins. The defanged Magpies, who will be without last season’s three first-choice central midfielders, looked fragile in pre-season, too. Their predicted Gameweek 1 XIs look ravaged and short on experience, so Bart Verbruggen, Virgil van Dijk, an out-of-position Maxim De Cuyper, Pascal Gross and Liverpool front two Florian Wirtz and Alexander Isak could prosper. I’d have much preferred the goal-hanging Jack Hinshelwood to Gross, incidentally, but budgetary restrictions hampered me. Were it not for Brighton’s trip to Tromso tonight, I’d be even more bullish about the Seagulls’ prospects.

Fulham and Crystal Palace, under new management, still look like works in progress from what I’ve seen of them in pre-season, hence the selection of Joao Pedro and Jordan Pickford. I feel like I could’ve gone bigger on both Chelsea and Everton, indeed.

Trusty striking steeds Erling Haaland and Igor Thiago, the 20+ goal forwards from 2025/26, round off this squad.
