Our team previews for Fantasy Premier League (FPL) 2026/27 continue with a focus on Fulham.

Here, we discuss their best FPL picks for the upcoming campaign, predict the line-up for Gameweek 1, review pre-season, detail their set-piece takers, assess the summer transfer activity and more.

REASONS TO BE CHEERFUL

FRESH IDEAS

Alvaro Arbeloa, the newly appointed head coach of Fulham, should bring some much-needed fresh ideas to Craven Cottage.

With a clear intent to play attacking football and provide opportunities for young players, the proposed adjustments sound pretty good, at least in theory.

Marco Silva did a decent job at Fulham, with finishes of 11th, 11th, 13th and 10th in his four Premier League seasons in charge, but it was still probably the right time for a change.

A fluid, possession-based approach under Arbeloa, likely in a 4-3-3 formation, could work well for Fulham’s Fantasy assets, then.

REASONS TO BE FEARFUL

This summer, Fulham have lost their two top goalscorers from 2025/26: Harry Wilson (£6.5m) and Raul Jimenez.

ARBELOA’S INEXPERIENCE

Arbeloa doesn’t have much experience in the hot seat.

Although he has spent some time with Real Madrid’s youth and reserve teams, he’s only had six months as a head coach.

How will Arbeloa handle a poor run of form, potentially in the first few weeks, given the fixtures, and is he a decent man-manager?

Time will tell, but it’s a clear concern as we head into the 2026/27 season, especially since Fulham perhaps don’t have the players to implement his attacking approach.

OPENING FIXTURES

As mentioned earlier, it’s the triple-header in Gameweeks 6-8 that whets the appetite. Fulham are the first team to play all three promoted clubs, and they do it all in a row.

Given the mostly tricky tests that precede this run, most FPL managers will be on a watching brief to see how a young-looking Cottagers side are faring under their equally inexperienced boss.

Gameweek 6 being a popular window for Wildcards will no doubt help swell the ownership figures.

PENALTY + SET-PIECE TAKERS

PENALTIES

This is the great unknown.

The Cottagers have waved goodbye to one of the most reliable penalty-takers in world football, Raul Jimenez, this summer. The Mexican hogged spot-kicks during his time in west London and in the last 18 months, only one other player has taken a Fulham penalty.

That player, strangely, was defender Antonee Robinson (£4.5m). The left-back converted from 12 yards in the Gameweek 37 draw with Wolverhampton Wanderers, a game that Jimenez missed.

Since then, Alex Iwobi (£5.5m) and Rodrigo Muniz – both of whom were in the side when Robinson netted in Gameweek 37 – scored from the spot in the August 1 friendly against Farense:

SET PIECES

With Wilson and Jimenez gone, Fulham have lost the two players responsible for 12 of their 13 direct free-kicks in 2025/26. Kevin (£5.5m) took the other, and we saw him on duty in pre-season.

Similarly, the outgoing Wilson and Lukic were the top two takers of corners for the Cottagers. Iwobi was third, and we saw him absorb a lot more of the responsibility in the recent friendlies. His assist in Fulham’s final warm-up game, indeed, came from a corner.

Kevin and Oscar Bobb (£5.5m) were also in the corner-taking mix.

This was the side that Arbeloa sent out for the final pre-season friendly against Stuttgart, although there was a behind-closed-doors match featuring other squad members on the same day.

Probably the main question mark is over Palacios, who is very inexperienced at senior level but who was thrust straight into the starting XI for what looked like the last two first-team friendlies. He is Arbeloa’s boy, however, having worked with him in Spain. Kevin is, of course, an alternative.

If you’re wondering why no Joachim Andersen (£5.0m) – he will miss Gameweek 1 as he completes a three-match ban carried over from last season.

FULHAM: ONES FOR THE WATCHLIST

We normally have separate ‘best players’ and ‘ones for the watchlist’ but with Fulham’s first five fixtures not great, a new manager at the helm, a young side being assembled, their DefCon-amassing centre-back suspended and the identity of the Cottagers’ penalty taker unknown, perhaps a mere ‘watchlist’ section will do here.

If Alex Iwobi (£5.5m) gets penalty-taking duties to go with an increased role at corners, and with a central midfield role theoretically boosting his DefCon potential, he’ll have a certain Gross-esque appeal about him. We’ve seen more than one Fulham fan questioning the Nigerian’s role under Arbeloa, however, so perhaps a bit of an observation period is prudent here.

Gonzalo Garcia (£5.5m) is definitely one to monitor. A £5.5m striker leading the line for a Premier League club, particularly one with a run like he enjoys in Gameweeks 6-8, piques our interest. Can he make the step up to regular senior-level football?

Joachim Andersen (£5.0m) hasn’t been handed as much of a price hike as the Senesis of this world, despite hitting the DefCon threshold in 60% of his outings in 2025/26. First, though, we’ll have to see whether Arbeloa-ball lands itself to defensive contributions – it certainly looked promising against Palace – and indeed if he waltzes straight back into the side after his ban.

Perhaps top of the intrigue list are the full-backs, who have been afforded a lot of attacking license in pre-season. Timothy Castagne (£4.5m), Antonee Robinson (£4.5m) and Ryan Sessegnon (£4.5m) racked up seven pre-season attacking returns between them, with the former on target in the final kickabout.

“Obviously you need to know your responsibilities – we cannot go every time, you need to pick your moments – but when we go, we have license to kill. We can go full, and both full-backs at the same time. That’s why Sess scored Wednesday from my cross, because we can both go in the box at the same time. It will be fun this season.” – Timothy Castagne

“Full-backs are very important for me in our style, in our game. They are doing a great job.” – Alvaro Arbeloa
