Our Fantasy Premier League (FPL) 2026/27 team previews continue with Daniel Farke’s Leeds United.

Here, we discuss their best FPL picks for the upcoming campaign, predict the line-up for Gameweek 1, review pre-season, detail their set-piece takers, assess the summer transfer activity and more.

REASONS TO BE CHEERFUL

BUCKING THE RELEGATION TREND IN AN IMPRESSIVE 2025/26 SEASON

Leeds had a really impressive first season back in the Premier League, which gives them a decent platform from which to build.

After a tricky start, the Whites turned it around and were officially safe from relegation with three matches to spare. After the struggles of newly promoted clubs in recent years, they – along with Sunderland – were a real success story.

Since then, three new players have arrived: James Trafford (£5.0m), who was wasted on the bench at Manchester City, DefCon magnet Tarik Muharemovic (£5.0m) and goal-grabbing Harry Wilson (£6.5m).

SOLID FOUNDATIONS

An underrated backline provided the foundation for much of Leeds’ success.

From the turn of the year, they were surprisingly ranked seventh for fewest goals conceded (24). They finished joint-fourth for clean sheets in that period, too, with six in 20 matches.

Could Trafford further improve the defence simply through stronger shot-stopping, too?

Karl Darlow and Lucas Perri (both £4.5m) both let in more league goals than Opta deemed they should have last season. Trafford was in the black, however. While there’s a small sample in Trafford’s case, Man City’s then cup goalkeeper also boasted a positive xGP figure in the FA Cup and EFL Cup.

With three of their first five games in 2026/27 on home turf, where they are typically strong, it feels like Leeds are in a good place.

REASONS TO BE FEARFUL

AWAY STRUGGLES

While Leeds held their own at Elland Road, particularly against sides ranked eighth or lower, they really struggled on the road.

There were just two away wins all season, one against an abject Wolverhampton Wanderers.

Even the addition of DefCon points couldn’t help any of their defenders to a better points-per-start average than 3.3 on their travels.

COMPETITION AT THE BACK!

Not a reason to be fearful of Leeds’ form – more a slight worry for owners of Joe Rodon (£4.5m) and Jaka Bijol (£5.0m)!

On Wednesday, the Whites secured the signing of Nico Elvedi, who usually features on the right or middle of central defence.

There’s no need for immediate panic; as Farke showed with Bijol last season, he’s happy to take his time with new arrivals and adopt the ‘if it ain’t broke, don’t fix it’ approach.

In effect, Elvedi is coming in as the new Sebastiaan Bornauw, the back-up stopper who has departed on loan, although the incoming Swiss international is perhaps a peg or two ahead of the man he’s replacing, ability-wise.

PENALTY + SET-PIECE TAKERS

PENALTIES

Dominic Calvert-Lewin (£6.0m) and Lukas Nmecha (£5.5m) were both in the spot-kick mix in 2025/26.

Nmecha took one with Calvert-Lewin on the field in November – and missed. Perhaps tellingly, in May, Calvert-Lewin took one with Nmecha on the field. That was the first time that had happened all season. Calvert-Lewin first in line, then?

In any event, it’s not often that both players will be on the field at the same time. So, whoever is leading the line, they’ll generally be responsible for the penalty if one is given.

“I was designated penalty taker today so if the ref pointed to the spot I knew it was going to be me.” – Dominic Calvert-Lewin, upon taking his first penalty of the season in December 2025 (via the Yorkshire Post)

SET PIECES

Anton Stach (£6.0m) was Leeds’ set-piece-taker extraordinaire in 2025/26. The taker of eight of their 10 direct free-kicks, he scored with three of them. Only Dominik Szoboszlai (£7.0m) could better that.

He also took the vast majority of their corners (over 100), with Sean Longstaff (£5.0m) having a brief run midway through the season before retreating from view.

New arrival Wilson has taken some corners in pre-season, and he himself was fond of a direct free-kick at Fulham.

LEEDS: PREDICTED FPL GAMEWEEK 1 LINE-UP

As was the case with many Premier League clubs, Leeds’ final pre-season line-up looked very much like what we’d expect to see in Gameweek 1. 

James Justin (£4.5m) continued at left wing-back in the absence of Gabriel Gudmundsson (£4.5m), who may miss the first two Gameweeks.

And with Noah Okafor (£6.0m) only recently back from a post-World Cup break, Brenden Aaronson (£5.5m) was used as the left-sided ’10’.

LEEDS: BEST FPL OPTIONS

Is the Tarik Muharemovic (£5.0m) hype getting a bit ‘OTT’ now? Perhaps, given that we’ve only seen him in meaningless friendlies, but he does have cult hero written all over him.

An eye-catching rate of DefCons at Sassuolo in 2025/26 was supplemented by four attacking returns, and we’ve seen him look menacing – and score a bicycle kick! – at set-piece situations in pre-season.

There’s little question that he’s going to be a regular starter, too. With many of the DefCon machines rising to £5.5m or £6.0m, the Bosnian could be a snip.

Joe Rodon (£4.5m) is a cheaper alternative and, interestingly, we’ve seen him rampaging up the right flank in pre-season, in a little tactical tweak from Farke. While he can’t compete with Muharemovic for DefCons, he did bag two assists this summer.

Anton Stach (£6.0m) has the disadvantage of not being on penalties, unlike Messrs Ndiaye, Le Fee and Gross, who are available at similar prices.

Nevertheless, a rudimentary assessment of value – last year’s points, this year’s prices – has him ahead of every other FPL midfielder.

Above: Helped by DefCon points and set-piece duties, Stach also averaged more points per start (4.9) than any current £6.0m-or-under FPL midfielder in 2025/26 (min. 10 starts)

Speaking of value, Dominic Calvert-Lewin (£6.0m) was the leading forward in the aforementioned article.

He can be frustratingly streaky: 12 of his 14 goals came either in Gameweeks 13-21 or Gameweeks 33-37. And he’s often profligate, underachieving on the xG front in nine of the last 10 seasons!

But he’s on penalties, the focus of everything Leeds do going forward, and probably playing his best football since 2020/21.

LEEDS: ONES FOR THE FPL WATCHLIST

Noah Okafor (£6.0m) hit a real purple patch towards the end of last season, scoring six goals in as many appearances.

A late return from World Cup duty likely means he’s starting 2026/27 on the bench, however, so he’s on ice for now.

Similarly, it’s watch and wait when it comes to Harry Wilson (£6.5m). He’s not quite hit his stride in pre-season, perhaps still getting used to his new surroundings, and the price rise no doubt dents his appeal.

We’ll also have to see how much he’ll be involved on set plays, with Stach such a dead-ball whizz.
