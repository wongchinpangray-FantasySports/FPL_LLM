REASONS TO BE CHEERFUL

KIND EARLY FIXTURES

Liverpool players will be hard to overlook in our Gameweek 1 squads.

That’s because the Reds have the best early fixture schedule, topping the ticker across their first five matches:

Andoni Iraola will make his bow as the Liverpool manager with an away trip to Newcastle United, before encounters with Nottingham Forest, Ipswich Town, Fulham and Bournemouth, all of whom will be under new management.

This run takes us up to the first international break of 2026/27 after Gameweek 5. This break lasts for three weeks, so it could be a perfect opportunity to Wildcard.

IRAOLA-BALL

Iraola did a superb job at Bournemouth, with a sixth-place finish and European qualification in 2025/26.

But it’s equally the style of football which offers so much optimism for the new campaign.

The Spaniard’s front-footed, attacking approach, which prioritises lots of pressing high up the pitch, is not only easy on the eye, but also highly effective.   

It could make Iraola a great fit at Liverpool, given the quality of the squad he will inherit.

Indeed, if Iraola can create a system and environment for Florian Wirtz (£7.5m) and Alexander Isak (£9.0m) to flourish, the Reds could be in for a very decent season.

REASONS TO BE FEARFUL

A PACKED SCHEDULE

Iraola will need to adjust to a packed schedule, however, which will include a UEFA Champions League campaign.

His proactive approach is fine for playing once a week, but how will it translate to a busier fixture list, with much shorter turnarounds and less time to prepare for each match?

There are other question marks, too – will Iraola’s style of play be as effective against teams who consistently sit deep, can he handle the pressure and intensity of the Anfield hot seat, etc?

LACK OF ATTACKING DEPTH

Liverpool have already recruited talented winger Victor Munoz (£6.5m) this summer.

But Kop legend Mohamed Salah has departed, Hugo Ekitike (£7.5m) is injured, while they also missed out on Yan Diomande, who decided to move to Real Madrid instead.

Yes, the Reds still have quality attackers, like Wirtz, Rio Ngumoha (£6.0m), Cody Gakpo (£7.0m) and Isak, but that alone probably won’t be enough to cope with next season’s demands, something Iraola discussed in his first Liverpool press conference.

“We have to accept the difficult situation right now. A lot of senior players leaving, very important players. Also, some of the very important players are injured: Ekitike, Bradley and Leoni. They are long-term injuries. In terms of improving the team, we have to consider replacing important players who were making important numbers and the players who will be missing time.” – Andoni Iraola

All five opponents are under new management, as are Gameweek 6 foes Manchester City.

PENALTY + SET-PIECE TAKERS

PENALTIES

Mohamed Salah is gone – so who steps up to take Liverpool’s penalties now?

Some assume it will be Dominik Szoboszlai (£7.0m). After all, the Hungarian took the only two Liverpool penalties that Salah wasn’t present for in 2025/26. One was scored, one missed.

However, Isak was off the field for both of them. In fact, he wasn’t on the field when any of the Reds’ spot-kicks were awarded last season. As a goal-getting striker, might the Swede pull rank?

Here are their historic records from 12 yards:

  • Szoboszlai: 20 scored from 22 attempts (90.1%)
  • Isak: 19 scored from 25 attempts (76%)

Szoboszlai has the career edge, then, but Isak has improved with age, scoring 13 of his last 15 efforts.

SET PIECES

Szoboszlai can at least confidently say he has free-kick-taking duties. No Premier League player took as many last season (14), and no one scored as many (four).

The Hungarian also took over 100 corners for the Reds. He was the main taker during pre-season but Cody Gakpo (£7.0m), who was more involved on set plays in the early stages of 2025/26 before taking a step back, did assist from a corner against Como on Sunday.

LIVERPOOL: PREDICTED FPL GAMEWEEK 1 LINE-UP

Liverpool sent out what looked very much like a first XI for their win over Como.

The key question mark is probably over how ready Jeremie Jacquet (£5.0m) is to start a competitive match. He was impressive on Sunday but that was his first action since February due to injury, and he was limited to 45 minutes. Ronald Araujo (£5.5m) is the deputy, if needs be.

Gakpo continues to be linked with a move elsewhere but keeps starting for the Reds regardless.

Ngumoha may have to start on the right, as he did against Como, but there is the option of moving Szoboszlai there and bringing in Alexis Mac Allister (£5.5m) – if he’s fit enough after a late return from the World Cup – in the middle. Munoz is another option on the right but his manager’s limited use of him in pre-season suggests bench duty for now.

LIVERPOOL: BEST FPL OPTIONS

Back fit after a miserable 2025/26, Alexander Isak (£9.0m) looks good to go for the new season. Coming through the World Cup unscathed, Isak encouragingly bagged two returns in Liverpool’s match against Monaco. He then hit the post against Como in his most recent outing.

44 goals, 10 assists and 383 points across two seasons with Newcastle United show what he is capable of. And now he’s had a starting price cut to a tempting £9.0m.

Penalty-taking duties are also up for grabs at Liverpool – although, as discussed above, there’s another pretender to that throne.

Regardless of spot-kicks, Isak is devoid of much competition, with Hugo Ekitike (£7.5m) out with a long-term injury, and he also has a favourable opening schedule.

Gameweek 6 might be a good hopping-off point, not only because of the fixture swing but also because Champions League football steps up then, and you wonder how Isak’s muscles withstand the rigours of two games a week.

Above: Isak was almost bang level with Erling Haaland for NPxGI in his last full season with Newcastle

As for the midfield, the stocks of Dominik Szoboszlai (£7.0m) and Florian Wirtz (£7.5m) have moved in opposite directions as the summer went on. Doubts over penalty-taking duties and a withdrawn midfield role under Iraola have slowed the hype around the Hungarian, whereas an advanced position almost alongside Isak – particularly against Monaco, when he racked up four shots – has spiked interest in Wirtz.

Szoboszlai (set plays, DefCon potential when deeper, possibly penalties) probably just edges it for now.

We know what we’re getting with Virgil van Dijk (£6.5m) at the back. Nailed, a set-piece threat and some level of DefCon potential (28 points last season), something that may even climb under Iraola. However…

LIVERPOOL: ONES FOR THE FPL WATCHLIST

…the £1.5m saving for Jeremie Jacquet (£5.0m) will be very hard to pass up, if he establishes himself as a regular. A superb debut against Como – a goal, a composed display and, pro rata, a threshold-hitting DefCon count – elevated interest in the young Frenchman.

He’s had just 45 minutes of action in six months, so it’s not totally guaranteed that he’ll a) start on Sunday and b) last the required hour. But, unquestionably, this young centre-half is one to watch in FPL terms.

Ongoing speculation over the future of Cody Gakpo (£7.0m) means we’ll park interest in him for now, even though he looks increasingly likely to start for the Reds in Gameweek 1.

We’ll also watch and wait to see what magic Iraola can do with Jeremie Frimpong (£5.5m). There’s no doubting his attacking potential – he delivered two assists in pre-season – but it remains to be seen whether the new gaffer fancies him long term at right-back, given the obvious defensive deficiencies.
